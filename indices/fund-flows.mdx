The [Fund Flows page](https://auramarkets.io/flows) (nav: **Markets → Fund Flows**) tracks net money moving into and out of ETFs — the hot thematic funds up top, then the whole cross-section: asset classes, sectors, regions, the levered complex, and crypto.

Unlike price, flow is a *decision*: someone chose to add or pull capital. auramarkets computes it from the primary source — **daily shares outstanding × NAV from the issuers themselves** (iShares, State Street, VanEck), which is exact to the creation unit. Funds whose issuers don't publish daily share counts use FactSet-derived series instead, and each fund's chart says which it is.

## The read

The front page of the tape — one state line and three lanes, all computed from the active lookback window and recomputed the moment you switch it:

- **LEAD** — the single most unusual flow on the tape right now: every tracked fund *and* the Bitcoin/Ether complexes compete, scored by how far the window's flow sits from that fund's own history (with a dollar floor, so a small fund's noisy reading can't headline). The winner gets the full treatment — dollars, sigma, its streak or largest-since record — plus the 13-week crowding clause when a theme complex has been absorbing money for a quarter.
- **ROTATION** — the question flows exist to answer: money *out of* what, *into* what. Nine exposure buckets (US core equity, bonds, cash, gold, semis/AI, tech/software, small caps, international, crypto) netted in dollars, top two per side.
- **WATCH** — the divergence flags (*exhaustion*: price still rising while money leaves; *capitulation*: outflows persisting deep into a drawdown), ranked by fund size, capped at three with a "+N more in the table" tail — the σ screener in the table below holds the full list.

The state line ("Flows running quiet" through "Extreme flows on the tape") is a severity tier derived from the Lead pick itself — it grades how unusual the biggest story is, so the verdict and its evidence can't disagree.

<Note>
Flow signals mean different things at different horizons, and the page is deliberate about this: short-window numbers (1 day to 1 month) are described, not editorialized — the research says short-term flow *follows* returns and continues. Only the slow, cumulated reads (13 weeks to a year of money piling into one theme) carry a cautionary tone, because that's the horizon where crowding has historically mattered.
</Note>

## One lookback, every panel

The pill row in the Hot Money header — **1W · 1M · 3M · YTD · 1Y · ✎** — sets the page's active window, and every panel follows it: the tiles' headline numbers and ordering, the Leverage Appetite pairs and their states, the Biggest Moves ranking, and (for 3M or custom) an extra column in the flows table. **1M is the default.** The ✎ pill takes any custom window from 2 to 520 weeks, computed live from each fund's weekly archive. Your selection is saved to your account.

## Hot Money

Tiles for the theme funds *you* care about — the defaults cover semis (SOXX, SMH, SOXL), software (IGV), memory (DRAM), tech (XLK), and the mega-cap complex (QQQ, MAGS), and the **+ Add** button opens a searchable picker (type a ticker or any part of a fund's name) to build your own set. Tiles arrange themselves by the active window's flow — strongest inflow first.

Each tile reads top to bottom the way a quote card does: the headline flow with a year of weekly history as a **sparkline right beside it**, a fixed **1W · YTD** reference row underneath, then — when a fund has earned one — its **read**, marked with a slim accent bar: the Flow Show superlative in plain sight ("Largest 13-week inflow since Jun 2025 · 4th straight week of inflows"). Long reads clip to two lines; hover for the full text. The bottom edge is pinned for the qualifiers.

A **% | $** pill beside the lookback ranks the tiles by flow as a share of assets or by dollars, and makes that measure the headline — the other sits under it, so a tile always shows both.

Every tile carries a **z-chip** on that bottom edge: the active window's flow scored against that fund's own history of the same window. Quiet readings sit in muted grey; past ±1.5σ the chip turns amber — the threshold controls emphasis, not existence, so "is this money unusual?" is answered at a glance on every tile. Badges appear beside it when earned:

- **EXHAUSTION** — price runs more than half a sigma *above* its history while flow runs half a sigma *below* it: the fund is still going up as money walks out. Hover the badge for the exact criteria.
- **CAPITULATION** — price and flow both more than a sigma below history, with the fund over 15% off its 1-year high: outflows persisting deep into the hole.
- **synthetic** — DRAM holds swaps and T-bills rather than only physical shares, so its flows behave differently.

## The flows table

Every tracked fund, grouped by tier, across the house timeframes: **1D · 1W · 1M · YTD · 1Y** — plus the active lookback as an extra sortable column whenever it isn't already one of those (3M, or your custom window). Percentages are *organic growth rates* — flow relative to the fund's assets at the window start — because a $1B flow means something entirely different to a $20B fund than to a $800B one. The strongest and weakest two in each column are tinted; click any column to sort, any row for that fund's full flow history as a chart.

Two controls in the table header turn it into a z-score screener:

- **% | σ** — flips every window column from flow percentages to **z-scores**, each window scored against that fund's own history of the same window. Heat past ±2σ, tooltips keep the underlying % and dollars, sorting works on the scores. (YTD sits out in σ mode — its window length drifts through the year, so a fair "vs own history" score doesn't exist.)
- **≥1σ** — hides every fund whose active-window flow is within a sigma of its own norm, leaving only the unusual money on screen. Forty-seven funds in, a handful of stories out.

## Leverage Appetite

The same exposure at 1x and 3x — whose door does the dip money walk through? Five pairs: **Semis** (SOXX/SMH vs SOXL), **Nasdaq 100** (QQQ vs TQQQ), **S&P 500** (SPY/IVV/VOO vs UPRO/SPXL), **Russell 2000** (IWM vs TNA), and **Long Treasuries** (TLT vs TMF). Each pair shows both sides' flow for the active window and earns a state:

Each row shows the window's flow as a share of assets, then the same flow in dollars.

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **CHASE** | the levered door taking money while the cash product is flat or selling — high-risk dip buying |
| **BROAD BID** | both risk settings taking money |
| **DE-RISK** | both risk settings shedding money |
| **UNWIND** | leverage coming off while the cash product holds its bid |

The headline line names the widest divergence — the day this panel shipped, it read *"Dip-buying is running through the levered door — SOXL +7.7% vs SOXX/SMH −0.8% of AUM (1M)."*

## Biggest Moves

The five biggest dollar inflows and outflows for the active window, as diverging bars — and the **spot Bitcoin and Ether ETF complexes compete in the same ranking** ("BTC ETFs" / "ETH ETFs" rows), because they're chasing the same marginal dollar. Click a fund's bar for its flow history; click a crypto row for the complex's.

## Crypto ETFs

Per-issuer daily flows for the spot Bitcoin and Ether complexes (source: Farside Investors), each card carrying:

- **D | W | M** pills — daily rows, calendar-week totals, or calendar-month totals, with the sparkline redrawing to match
- **‹ ›** paging — walk back through the full history (Bitcoin to January 2024) with the window fixed at ten rows
- a **Zσ row** pinned above the table — each issuer's latest reported day scored against its own history, plus a complex-level score in the Total column
- an evidence line in the Flow Show grammar — *"6th straight day of inflows"*, *"Largest daily outflow since…"*
- clickable issuer tickers (IBIT, FBTC, ETHA…) opening the standard stock pop-out

Click anywhere on a card for the complex's full history chart, with the spot BTC/ETH price as an overlay.

## The history chart

Every fund row, tile, and crypto card opens the same chart: net-flow bars with two optional overlay lines on the right-hand scale — **Price** (the fund's NAV where the issuer publishes it, a price series otherwise — the subtitle says which) or **Σ Flow**, the cumulative net dollars since the history began. One overlay at a time; they don't share units.

- **D | W | M** switches the bar granularity — daily (trailing three years), weekly, or monthly (both full-depth) — and the range presets rescale so "1Y" always means one year of bars
- **1Y / 3Y / 5Y / Max** presets, plus drag-zoom and pan
- History reaches as far as the records go: SPY and the sector funds to 2006, the major iShares funds to ~2000, EWJ to 1996

## Where the numbers come from

| Layer | Source | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| iShares funds (SOXX, IGV, IVV, TLT, HYG…) | BlackRock daily records | exact shares outstanding, back to inception |
| SPDR funds (SPY, sectors, GLD) | State Street daily records | exact shares outstanding, back to 2006 |
| SMH, GDX | VanEck daily records | derived from AUM and NAV, back to 2011 |
| QQQ, MAGS, DRAM, VOO, JEPQ, URA + the levered complex (SOXL, TQQQ, UPRO, SPXL, TNA, TMF, BITX, ETHU) and ETHA | FactSet-derived series | daily net flows, up to ~11 years |
| Bitcoin / Ethereum ETF complexes | Farside Investors | per-issuer daily, since launch |
| Price overlays for FactSet-sourced funds and crypto | market close series | adjusted closes standing in for NAV |

Data refreshes several times a day as issuers post their files (typically next-morning for the prior session). A handful of data-quality guards run automatically — share-count jumps from fund splits are excluded (a split doubles shares without moving a dollar), issuer misprints are cross-checked against published assets, young funds don't print percentages against seed capital, and any window reaching into a fund's incubation era shows a dash instead of a thousand-percent artifact.
